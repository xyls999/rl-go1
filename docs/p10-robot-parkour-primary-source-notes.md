## What actually produces a jump

- **Task and observation.** The paper learns five separate simulation teachers (`climb`, `leap`, `crawl`, `tilt`, `run`). The **leap teacher** receives 29-dimensional proprioception (roll, pitch, body angular velocity, joint positions/velocities), previous 12-dimensional action, recurrent GRU state, *privileged* forward-obstacle distance, height/width and a four-category obstacle-type one-hot vector, plus privileged physical parameters. It outputs **12 joint-position targets**, not a prescribed foot trajectory or a takeoff command. Obstacles in leap training have gap lengths **0.20–0.80 m** and evaluation gaps **0.30–0.90 m**; the simulated gap is **0.8 m across and 0.8 m deep**. The leap specialist's fixed commanded forward speed is **1.5 m/s**, distinct from the general “around 1 m/s” description and from other specialist speed commands. [PDF pp. 4, 16–17]
- **Deployable perception and policy.** Privileged type, dimensions and physics are **teacher-only**. In simulation, known current obstacle type selects the appropriate teacher to label states; DAgger trains one recurrent student from *student-visited states* against teacher actions in hard-collision environments. The deployed student takes proprioception, previous action and CNN features of the robot's forward-facing **48×64 depth image**; it uses a **256-unit GRU**, 128-dimensional visual embedding and MLP hidden sizes **512/256/128**. This is learned, memory-based visual obstacle recognition/skill selection, **not** an actor supplied with simulator gap labels or a runtime selector between five privileged teachers. In simulation ablations, a blind student had **0%** leap success, an MLP student **1%**, the full visual student **80%**, and the privileged oracle **82%**, under the reported harder evaluation protocol. [PDF pp. 5–8, 18]
- **Motion prior versus imitation.** The abstract explicitly says **no reference-motion data**. DAgger is **teacher-action distillation**, not imitation of animal motion capture or a demonstration library; the paper contrasts itself with motion imitation. It does **not** specify a hand-engineered jump trajectory, explicit crouch/takeoff/ballistic-flight/landing phases, a contact-state switch, target landing foot placements, or a separate deployment-time leap gate. The positive description is a recurrent joint-target policy trained for crossing a forward gap with reward, curriculum and teacher distillation. Thus crossing results do not establish any particular contact sequence or a reusable takeoff/landing state machine. [PDF pp. 1–2, 4–6, 16–18]

## Training signal and physical realization

- **Reward/curriculum.** All specialists share forward-speed tracking (including lateral and yaw behavior), a joint mechanical-power penalty, and a per-step **alive reward of 2**; soft-collision pretraining additionally penalizes obstacle penetration measured by samples inside robot collision bodies (more around vulnerable hip/shoulder motors), with penetration terms multiplied by forward speed to discourage simply charging through. A per-environment automatic curriculum begins at difficulty score **s=0**, adjusts it by **±0.05** at reset using the previous episode's mean penetration reward against a threshold, caps at **1**, and interpolates easy/hard obstacle parameters. PPO first trains with *penetrable* obstacles and `r_skill + r_penetrate`, then fine-tunes with **hard physical collisions** and `r_skill` alone and randomized obstacle sizes. Running skips soft-collision pretraining. The PDF gives no separate leap-specific takeoff, flight-time, touchdown, or landing-stability reward, and does not specify a reproducible leap-specific **termination/contact-failure condition** in these method and appendix descriptions. Table 4's printed coefficients should be checked against the original page image before numerical reuse because the translated text layer loses superscripts. [PDF pp. 4–5, 16–18]
- **Simulation and deployment timing.** Training uses Isaac Gym (appendix: **Preview 4**); randomization covers mass/COM, motor strength, friction, camera pose/FOV and observation delays. Simulator/real camera processing addresses depth noise, holes and temporal/spatial filtering. Depth updates at **10 Hz**, while the deployed policy runs at **50 Hz**, reusing the latest CNN feature; a roughly **1 kHz PD** stage uses `Kp=50`, `Kd=1` and **25 N·m** commanded torque clipping. Hardware uses forward-facing **Intel RealSense D435**, **Jetson NX**, and protective tilt/torque limits and remote emergency stop. These are the paper's hardware/timing specifications, not tested settings for this repository's controller. [PDF pp. 5–6, 17, 19–20]
- **Validation/species scope.** Reported **A1** indoor tests (10 trials per skill/difficulty) include **80%** success over a **0.60 m** gap (~1.5 body lengths); the paper also reports outdoor visual parkour examples including a **0.6 m** gap between stone benches (the cited outdoor passage does not identify which robot). The simulated five-skill evaluation covers **100 trials and three seeds** with **80% student leap** and **82% oracle leap** success in its reported table. Authors say the same training pipeline generalizes to **A1 and Go1**, both equipped with Jetson NX and D435, but the quantified indoor 0.60 m jump result is explicitly **A1**, and the appendix's detailed physical setup is A1; the PDF does **not** provide a Go1-specific jump success percentage, Go1 leap checkpoint, or evidence for this repository's Go1 v9 configuration. [PDF pp. 2–3, 6, 8, 19–20]

## Boundary against the local Go1 interface

The existing `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-15-go1-v9-gap-contact-leap-tire-transition-design.md` is read **only** for local interface constraints: its v9 actor retains **1073 observations** without simulator geometry truth; short gaps (**≤0.24 m**) remain a walking task; long-gap leap uses a separate unvalidated checkpoint/interface (`trench_state/gate_action`) and explicit handoff; the project commands **0.35–0.8 m/s**, and contact/landing criteria and mixed-course evaluation are local requirements.
