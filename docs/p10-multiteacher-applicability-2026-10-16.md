## What the paper actually transfers

- **Five separate privileged simulation RL specialists** (`climb`, `leap`, `crawl`, `tilt`, `run`) learn 12 joint-target actions, with proprioception, previous action, recurrent state and privileged obstacle geometry/type and physical parameters. The leap specialist's forward target is **1.5 m/s**, not the general ~1 m/s or this project's 0.35–0.8 m/s. Leap train gaps span **0.20–0.80 m**, evaluation gaps **0.30–0.90 m**; the modeled gap is 0.8 m deep. [PDF pp. 4, 16–17]
- Specialists first use **soft, penetrable obstacle dynamics** with speed-weighted penetration penalty and adaptive obstacle difficulty; PPO then fine-tunes in **hard collision** physics without penetration reward. Running skips the soft stage. This is teacher acquisition, not student distillation, and cannot be replaced by simply raising isolated-skill scores. [PDF pp. 4–5, 16–17]
- One **visual recurrent student** is trained by DAgger in hard-collision simulation: run the **student** to collect its visited states, use the simulation's known obstacle type to select the relevant specialist to label actions, aggregate labels and train the student. Its deployed inputs are proprioception, prior action and features of a **48×64 forward depth image**, not teacher-only geometry/type/physics. Appendix specifies 128-D CNN embedding, **GRU256**, MLP **512/256/128**; deployment depth updates at 10 Hz and policy at 50 Hz. No deployment-time privileged teacher selector. This is action distillation, not reference-motion imitation or an explicit takeoff/landing phase machine. [PDF pp. 1–2, 5–6, 18]
- The paper reports simulated hard evaluation of the unified student (100 trials/three seeds in its table), including **80% leap versus 82% privileged oracle**, not universal ≥90%; blind and feedforward MLP leap ablations are **0% and 1%** respectively. Its quantified physical **0.60 m gap, 80% of 10 trials** is explicitly **A1**, not a Go1 jump success result. Both A1 and Go1 appear as platform scope, but no Go1-specific leap percentage or checkpoint for this project's observation/action schema is established. [PDF pp. 6–8, 19; also primary-source notes, “Validation/species scope”]

## Applicability to proposed Go1 sequence

“Bring each non-`combined` terrain family to ≥90%, then distill multiple teachers into one **1073-observation / 12-action / GRU128** Go1 student” is a **reasonable project hypothesis, not a faithful reproduction or validated consequence** of P10. P10 has **five skill teachers**, not necessarily one per local eleven terrain families; it gives no isolated-≥90% prerequisite, no theorem that isolated competence composes, no 1073-D height/confidence-map student or GRU128 equivalence, and its leap speed, perception and robot-specific quantitative evidence differ. Local 1073/12/GRU128 and prohibition on privileged actor inputs are project constraints (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-16-go1-v11-unified-roster11-design.md`, lines 9–10); simulated height rays are not automatically a real depth camera. Preserve the local speed and observation contract rather than borrowing the paper's 1.5 m/s leap target. P10's teachers receive privileged geometry, but the current project's stricter rule does **not** automatically permit that for any policy actor, including a training-only teacher. Keep teacher and student actor inputs observable-only unless the owner explicitly changes that rule; privileged truth may be used in the training reward/critic and an offline training label selector, never in deployment or actor inputs.

**Mixed-transition data are indispensable at the distillation stage.** Student-visited trajectories must include approaches, takeoff/landing and recoveries, short-to-long-gap distinctions, transitions into/out of tire fields, repeated obstacles and complete `combined` routes, with the correct teacher labeling each encountered state. Merely pooling successful isolated rollouts or teacher demonstrations misses the student's off-distribution states and compound failures; isolated ≥90% is at best a teacher-quality screen, not evidence of mixed-course success. If only a specialist executes its own isolated terrains, there is no label coverage for ambiguous boundaries or recovery after imperfect transitions; acquire and evaluate those trajectories with hard physics and DAgger, and explicitly resolve conflicting teachers/undefined labels. P10's type-based simulation labeling presupposes a known obstacle category during **training**; how to label overlapping local families and compound states is an open design decision, not specified by P10. [PDF pp. 4–6; local diagnosis `docs/v11-improvement-research-2026-10-16.md`, “结论与证据界限” and “隔离版…”]

**Current counterevidence:** local v11 same-plan gate reports `combined` **0/273**, `gaps_long` **47/92**, `gaps_short` **47/53**, `tire_field` **38/47**; the command-compliant replay on *frozen* old weights worsened long gaps to **3/238** and short gaps to **16/144**. The old family-conditioned 1.8× speed path is not an admissible student/teacher-selection shortcut; simply removing it without retraining is not proof that compliant learning is impossible. The local 90%-per-family criterion is a proposed acceptance gate, not a P10 result. No training, simulation, production modification or Go1 capability claim is made here. **Uncertainties / non-inferences:** The supplied PDF is a side-by-side translation rather than independently verified publisher typesetting; OCR/text-layer equations and some table typography are lossy. The reported A1 indoor trials, simulation leap table and platform generalization do not bound performance of this Go1 policy or certify physical jumping, contact detection, landing or safe mixed routes. No evidence here shows whether the current 1073-D representation resolves all relevant gaps or whether any candidate teacher can attain ≥90% under compliant commands. P10 does not specify a general transition-label conflict resolver, nor demonstrate ≥90% across this project's 11 families.
